# Teaching quality contract, version 1

Applies to aptitude, reasoning, English, Python, SQL, DSA, system design, AI/GenAI/RAG/agents and interviews. A section template or word count cannot establish quality. Review the actual candidate, including its answers, fixtures, provenance and versioned competency references. Technical review must be independent of the author. Agent-generated text is never evidence of a human review.

For each approved beginner lesson, the reviewer must establish:

1. **Objective:** a learner can explain what they will learn and what prior knowledge is needed.
2. **Accurate explanation:** the explanation is simple, precise and factually supported. Analogies have stated limits.
3. **Terminology:** terms are explained before use; unnecessary jargon is removed.
4. **Worked example:** a concrete example states its inputs, assumptions and correct result.
5. **Reasoning:** intermediate steps explain why the result follows, including units or execution state where relevant.
6. **Mistakes:** common misconceptions are linked to useful checks or counterexamples.
7. **Progressive practice:** tasks increase in difficulty and demand application rather than repeating the prose.
8. **Worked answers:** every answer and rubric is independently checked. A keyword match cannot establish semantic correctness.
9. **Hints:** hints reveal progressively useful guidance without immediately giving away the answer.
10. **Revision:** a concise summary supports later recall without claiming that reading creates mastery.
11. **Provenance:** factual references and redistribution permission are distinct; copied/adapted material retains the applicable notices and verified upstream identity.
12. **Independent technical review:** a named human checks correctness, completeness and beginner accessibility against the exact validation digest.

The machine-readable checklist additionally records semantic-answer correctness, reviewed coverage, and statement/judge agreement. These are explicit human judgments. A false or absent checklist entry blocks approval. The template starts with every item false.

Subject-specific review should check mathematical units and rounding in aptitude; satisfiability and answer uniqueness in reasoning; usage conventions and valid variants in English; mutation, return types and exceptions in Python/DSA; duplicate/NULL/order behavior in SQL; assumptions and tradeoffs in design; dated factual references and limitations in AI; and honest, unscored reflection boundaries in communication. Open responses cannot acquire an objective grade through this publication checklist.

Contextual Python/SQL fragments must say which setup is missing and why they are not executable. Review must verify them manually or require a complete fixture. Never describe those fragments as executed. Reserved transfer/recall pools, mastery-policy changes and the nine-subject curriculum are later phases.
